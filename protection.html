MIRL Lacuna

what this protects, and what it does not
Material / Image Research LabUniversity of California, Santa Barbara

A Lacuna register can carry risk: the identities of people who remember, the locations of unsafe sites, testimony given in confidence. This page states plainly where your data lives, what the tool protects, and where its protection ends, so that trust rests on facts rather than reassurance.

Where the register lives

The page is static. It has no server, no accounts, and no analytics, and it receives nothing: the hosted copy is exactly as private as one you run yourself. Your register exists in at most three places, all of them yours: this browser's own storage (the autosave), a .json project file if you save one or choose Keep the file on disk, and any exports you make. The offline copy the tool keeps of itself, through a service worker, caches the software, never your data.

What leaves the machine

Four actions send something out. Each is taken by you; none is automatic.

  • Look up online, on the atlas, sends the typed place name to OpenStreetMap's geocoder. The bundled offline gazetteer, the default, sends nothing.
  • Opening a surviving copy at a web or IIIF address fetches that address.
  • Fetch and hash, on an evidence web address, fetches that address to fingerprint it.
  • The Wayback button asks the Internet Archive to save a public web address, in a new tab.

Everything else, every keystroke of cataloguing, geocoding against the bundled gazetteer, the hashing of local evidence files, and the making of every export, happens on your machine.

The lock

Locking the register encrypts the file at rest: the browser autosave, the live disk file, and saved project files, with AES-GCM under a key derived from your passphrase (PBKDF2, 600,000 rounds). Anyone who later opens this browser, or picks up the .json, finds an encrypted envelope and nothing else.

What the lock does not cover: the open session (while the register is on the screen, it is readable on the screen), the machine itself (a compromised browser, extension, or operating system can read what you can), and a passphrase that is guessed, shared, or coerced. A lost passphrase is lost; there is no recovery, by design. Exports are publications and are never encrypted.

Shared or borrowed machines

The browser autosave outlives the tab. On a library or lab computer, the next person at the same browser profile could open your register unless it is locked. When you leave a machine that is not yours, use Project → Forget on this device: it clears the autosave, the session's key, and the remembered file connection, and leaves the tool empty. Files you saved yourself are untouched, and opening one brings the register back.

What exports reveal by design

Every export passes the consent boundary: entries held back or struck, restricted and embargoed evidence, withheld sightings, source identities, the investigation log, and withheld places do not travel. Two things are said aloud on purpose. The finding aid states how many further entries are recorded and not published, because a catalogue of absence should not hide its own. And an approximate place publishes rounded to about ten kilometres, which conceals an address but not a region; if even the region is unsafe to name, keep the place withheld.

Publication is a one-way door. Once a finding aid or data file is shared, its contents are in the world's hands, not the register's.

What this cannot promise

Lacuna is a browser tool, and honest about it. It cannot protect against a compromised or surveilled device. It cannot revoke what has been published. It does not strip identifying metadata from images you circulate by other means; prepare an image with a dedicated redaction tool before sharing it. Before each release, the consent boundary is tested against a register built to catch leaks: the proof sheet proves that nothing marked restricted, withheld, or held back reaches any export.

Where safety depends on more than software, on law, on counsel, on community protocols, use those. When the stakes include life or liberty, treat this tool as one layer of protection, never the only one.
